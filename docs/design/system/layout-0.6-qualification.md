Updated 8 November 2026. The owner requested a Trinity specification, common
ELK/Native/Trinity interface, metrics, state/invariant ownership and plan cleanup.
The owner subsequently authorized the ten-stage roadmap, with a report after each
stage. Stages 1–3 are implemented for the neutral contract, first Requirement
consumer and legacy completion split. Stage 4 adds a bounded private flat kernel;
stage7 now registers its bounded Requirement and rectangular primary adapters publicly. Other families,
compound support, broader equivalent public costs and adoption remain unfinished. Automation stays off. Defaults, versions, original
limits and Replacement24 remain unchanged.

## Starting position

The routed kernel is bounded to64nodes/128edges and explicit measured
paint/contact support. Requirement and flat rectangular Flowchart/State are publicly selectable. Existing ELK/Native runtime
products are preserved; new common validation is not a visible-correctness gain. At `f55a8f8ec`, Native's known
contacts and Sparse32 proof exhaustion remain. ER256 delivery is already repaired
through both backends; option validation is already a leaf module. Neither is an
unfinished task to repeat. Runtime CI remains failed; this design makes no readiness
claim. Latest exact-head status belongs in the PR, not an expanding plan history.

The specification's metric table links the published Trident baseline and focused
Class128 supplement. Those retain partial fidelity, unknown ink, mixed cached
costs and growing retention. Pele source is recovered; a comparable Pele runtime /
actual-ink ranking remains unexecuted. No general replacement claim follows.

Preserve useful checks and evidence while cutting repetition. Geometry freeze,
actual-write capture, reentrant publication, mutable-input isolation, typed
fallback and cumulative budgets protect real failures; they are not ceremony.
