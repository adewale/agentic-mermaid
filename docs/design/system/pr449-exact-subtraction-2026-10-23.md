## RSS attribution: a confirmed instrument limitation

The latest #433 feedback identifies a repeated process high-water floor. A separate frozen experiment now checks its semantics directly: two rounds, zero/192 MiB parent page pressure, direct `execve`/Python `Popen(start_new_session=True)`, and a fixed 64 MiB Node allocation. All eight probes complete once within their bounds. No renderer is imported.

With 192 MiB parent pressure, both launch methods report Node `maxRSS` around **201 MiB** before and after the child allocation, while Node's current-mm `VmHWM` rises from about **28 to 96 MiB**. Zero-pressure Popen controls instead rise from 27.875 to 95.5–95.75 MiB. Popen therefore does not universally isolate this statistic from parent-memory history here. The zero-pressure direct-exec floor was already present in the Python parent; its earlier origin was not observed.

Historical RSS ratios remain recorded observations, **not isolated renderer footprint gains**. This does not identify the origin of old floors, justify subtracting them or invalidate separately measured allocation totals. A future footprint protocol must distinguish worker-start/pre-import/current RSS, current-mm peak and process-lifetime high-water values. Linux status readings are approximate. The maintained-worker work belongs to #471. Primary references: [pinned Node24.19 resource usage](https://nodejs.org/download/release/v24.19.0/docs/api/process.html#processresourceusage), [getrusage](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/getrusage.2.html), and [proc status](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man5/proc_pid_status.5.html).

## Current routing: fewer crossings, too many long paths

A frozen offline diagnostic reads all four existing ec72 known-source captures: 1,209 inputs × two engines × two cache policies. There are 1,202 matched successful inputs per policy; the same seven ELK failures remain. Geometry is identical across the cache policies, so they are not independent samples. Native paint remains 223 passed / 986 unverified; ELK has 220 passed / 982 unverified / seven failures. No new renderer or validator ran.

| Metric, matched current inputs, one policy | Native | ELK |
|---|---:|---:|
| Visible edges | 31,454 | 31,454 |
| Existing crossing count | 9,330 | 30,414 |
| Actual geometric bends | 71,057 | 71,634 |
| Routes with at least seven stored points | 1,898 | 538 |
| Routes with at least ten stored points | 531 | 22 |
| Maximum stored points in one route | 26 | 12 |

The [official Mermaid layout-makers guide](https://mermaid.ai/open-source/community/layout-makers-guide.html) suggests examining nonlinear per-edge complexity. Applying a guide-inspired diagnostic to stored-point counts gives native a higher penalty on **653/1,202 inputs**, a lower one on 361 and a tie on 188. Three 26-point native paths contribute 93.88% of its total penalty. This is **not** Mermaid's full hard validator or its final score: raw points can include harmless subdivisions, and crossing reductions cannot waive geometry failures. Exact integer arithmetic is retained without a cap. These results justify investigating route construction and simplification before another tiny CPU-owner optimization; they do not qualify a routing-policy change.

A separately labelled exploratory supplement checks those three paths against the existing geometric-bend convention and original coordinates. Each really has **24 direction changes**, with zero duplicates, collinear subdivisions or reversals. Their edge indices are 7, 52 and 148 in the labelled 120-node mesh. Straight-line point cleanup offers no improvement there. A useful next experiment must change geometry while preserving ports, labels, obstacles, crossings and proof; the existing shortening pass and its eligibility/budget guards need inspection first. This three-path result does not generalise to every long route.
