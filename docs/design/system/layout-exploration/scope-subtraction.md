# PR449: scope and subtraction exploration

## Decision

Keep ELK as the default, keep native selection explicit, and treat smaller supported cohorts as an option worth measuring. The present evidence does **not** justify replacing ELK across six families, or asserting that Flowchart-only is the best first native scope. Flowchart contains the most substantial native visual improvements **and all six observed adverse uncached cyclic allocation cases**. State and Requirement are the cleanest small native pilot by source geometry; Architecture is the clearest family to quarantine until its final routed output improves. ER needs investigation of duplicated label ownership before a removal decision.

Do not expand family count to improve a headline coverage number. The other built-in families already use different layout mechanisms; they do not presently fetch ELK through this selector. Expansion would add semantic obligations without automatically retiring an existing dependency.

This is an exploration note, not an implementation change or a new benchmark. Counterfactual scopes below reuse paired observations and assume excluded families select ELK; they are **not measurements of rebuilt reduced-scope artifacts**, and not evidence of the resulting mixed-session cache/latency behavior.

## Shared costs: family subtraction is not sixfold solver subtraction

All six native integrations load the same optional producer implementation (`src/layout/agentic-layered.lazy.ts:5–18`). It exposes `candidate`, `compound` and `graph`, backed by `agentic-producer-core.ts` and the shared generic core. Family-specific drawing and adapters remain needed for ELK output. Disabling a family does not necessarily tree-shake its renderer or eliminate the native chunk when another family needs it.

Removing one native family cannot remove this shared pipeline or the solver still needed by other native families.

A narrowed policy that sends a second family to ELK can fetch **both** producers in the same realm. Scope byte savings must therefore be measured as the emitted file/request union for representative sessions, not summed family deltas. Test cold first eligible family, cold first excluded family, native→excluded, excluded→native, and typed native refusal with explicit fallback. Source simplifications that cause chunk reorganization need closure requalification, not optimistic source-line accounting.

The peer timing gap also warns against attributing everything to the solver: normal local Flowchart5 is native7.72ms vsELK6.55ms; Sequence20 is11.8 vs12.1 and C4 is10.2 vs10.2 even though those latter families use no new generic graph solver. Much faster peer implementations on Sequence/C4 demonstrate a pipeline/product-contract investigation opportunity; narrowing generic graph families cannot directly repair those paths. Profiling and equivalent output contracts are required before assigning that gap to any particular feature.

## Native structural scope: how to avoid an oracle policy

If narrower native inputs are tested, use a declarative predicate on the parsed measured model, not a list of observed failures:

1. Define graph topology precisely: directed forest; directed acyclic graph; or arbitrary graph. Decide whether weakly connected forest, fan-in and multiedges qualify, and distinguish self-loops from two-edge cycles.
2. Define compound exclusions separately: nested groups, cross-scope relationships, frame endpoints, ancestor endpoints and mixed group directions. Acyclicity does not solve container semantics.
3. Keep active rank/adjacency/short-edge hints, ambiguous identities and unbounded search failures as typed native refusals. With `fallback:'none'`, they must stay failures rather than silently fetching ELK.
4. Preserve an input-size/point/operation contract independently of topology. A huge DAG with many long labelled edges can be harder than a small cycle.
5. Define transitions before editing tests: insertion/deletion that crosses the predicate can switch the entire placement engine. Measure unaffected-node motion, frame movement and undo determinism, and emit decision provenance. Do not introduce stateful backend hysteresis without explicit stable input/session semantics.
6. Classify both current246 sources and fresh generated/negative family workloads through the parser. This exploration intentionally reports unknown tree/DAG coverage instead of pretending Mermaid source-text heuristics are a correct classifier.

A product may keep six adapters as opt-in capability while documenting a narrower recommended cohort; that is cheaper and reversible than deleting support before usage/profile evidence exists. It still owes truthful strict-native admission and cannot advertise a26-source pilot as a246-source ELK replacement.

## What subtraction must not mean

Do not delete authored relationships, labels, group boundaries, architecture port sides, active hints or concurrency semantics to make a layered model easy. Do not convert checker defects into ordinary fallback, replace unverified certificates with a passing label, discard expensive failure cases from totals, or remove safety/caps from the timed path while retaining the old product claim. Those would change the task rather than solve it.

The best current subtraction hypotheses remove repeated decisions over the **same** final meaning: one label owner, a placement-only contract where paths are disposable, one proof per immutable geometry generation and one intentional cache ownership boundary. They have concrete code support but await empirical attribution and fresh holdout validation.
