# What Beautiful Mermaid and Zombie Mermaid users actually had trouble reading

**The strongest risk is that a passing crossing score can hide which relationship a line represents.** A wallet analyst supplied a diagram where a shared bus made two transfers appear possible that the source never declared. A second user supplied a seven-stage workflow where the sequence of work became hard to follow. The current Agentic Mermaid (AM) output separates the disputed wallet branch and preserves the workflow. The wallet still has three distinct transfers arriving through one visible arrowhead. Another concrete precedent comes from Zombie Mermaid (ZM), which shipped a crossing repair that cost one extra terminal row. These examples identify a limit of strict dimension-by-dimension acceptance and a reason to keep protecting readable edge ownership. They establish no new hard AM failure. [Wallet report](https://github.com/lukilabs/beautiful-mermaid/issues/52) · [Workflow report](https://github.com/lukilabs/beautiful-mermaid/issues/83) · [ZM’s accepted repair](https://github.com/dfadler/zombie-mermaid/pull/2859)

## 1. A real wallet investigation with zero crossings

**Beautiful Mermaid (BM) #52, opened February 27, 2026 and still open.** Leslie555 posted the original ten-node, eleven-transfer LR graph, circled the confusing part in green and linked a drawing they could read. In the source, TWnK4k, TQBb8D and TUaYgu all pay the upper HuionePay wallet. Only TUaYgu pays the lower one. In the BM screenshot the three intermediaries feed a nearly continuous vertical bus branching toward both wallets. In the author-preferred screenshot, three separate arrows arrive at the upper wallet and only the TUaYgu arrow enters the lower one. The reporter also opened the still-open BM PR #57 for a merge control. ZM exposed the SVG control in #49 and later demonstrated both settings on the original source. [Issue and both original screenshots](https://github.com/lukilabs/beautiful-mermaid/issues/52) · [Reporter’s PR](https://github.com/lukilabs/beautiful-mermaid/pull/57) · [Fork’s exact-source comparison](https://github.com/lukilabs/beautiful-mermaid/issues/52#issuecomment-6041364162)

**Current same-source evidence.** AM default and balanced produce the same SVG, with all eleven occurrences, zero proper crossings, zero soft label conflicts, zero reported parallel/opposing overlaps and passed paint. TUaYgu’s line to the lower wallet is straight at y = 199.775. Its line to the upper one leaves at y = 193.625 and turns upward. The lower wallet’s exclusive source is now traceable. The three upper-wallet routes still share their final 12.375 px and marker position. Fresh ZM 5.18.0 default gives those three transfers separate arrival points at y = 179, 197 and 215. BM 1.1.3, ZM 5.18.0 and both AM modes all have zero crossings. Each drawing answers the question “who pays whom?” with different amounts of work. The reporter has expressed no judgment on AM’s rendition. [Written shafts and hashes](raw/real-issue-product-inspection.json) · [Original marked image](raw/bm52-original.png) · [Author-preferred image](raw/bm52-author-preferred.png)

**Why this matters to selection.** This source has no edge captions or duplicate source-target pairs. A certified incumbent with zero crossings and zero label conflicts skips AM’s automatic unbundled and balanced-order comparisons. Proper crossings exclude collinear shafts. The reported overlap categories cover opposing ink and same-endpoint parallel routes. They do not enumerate same-direction sharing between different sources feeding the same target. A similar chart could therefore retain confusing ink through both modes. The upstream user requested a switch, and the current AM API exposes no equivalent general SVG merge toggle. One shared arrowhead can still faithfully represent an ordinary convergence, as the next examples show. ## 2. A user deliberately preferred a taller retry workflow

**BM #83, opened April 2, 2026 and closed October 9 by its reporter.** Jiezhengj supplied a 34-node, 44-edge TD workflow. Six stage-local retries and five returns from H3 to earlier modules must read backwards while successful work advances. The reporter measured 3,662.35 × 650.30 px and preferred 983.93 × 3,372.70 px after a group of ELK changes. That increases height 5.19× and area 39.34%. A second participant supplied a ten-step pull-request workflow with its own retry. The fork maintainer reproduced the large case on BM 1.1.3 and ZM 4.2.0, with the latter at about 988 × 3,373 px. The reporter’s proposed settings included forced model order, and the fork attributed its result to broader cycle handling. The issue cannot isolate a single ELK setting. [Issue and measurements](https://github.com/lukilabs/beautiful-mermaid/issues/83) · [Second workflow](https://github.com/lukilabs/beautiful-mermaid/issues/83#issuecomment-4744036064) · [Versioned visual comparison](https://github.com/lukilabs/beautiful-mermaid/issues/83#issuecomment-6041376503)

**Current same-source evidence favors AM’s default.** Both AM modes produce identical 1,074.186 × 3,899.55 px SVGs. All 33 primary links advance downward by node centers, all eleven authored returns go upward, all 18 edge captions survive, proper crossings and soft label conflicts are zero, and paint passes. Fresh BM 1.1.3 has 34 proper crossings and the short, wide layout. Fresh ZM 5.18.0 has zero and a tall layout. The user’s original before/after dimensions demonstrate why a taller correction could be unreachable from a poor incumbent under the balanced rule. For this actual input AM already produces the intended ordering before optional selection. **Insertion order can also mislead.** BM #65 and #68, both open since March 18 and filed by the same reporter, give working TD ASCII inputs. In #65, writing A → C before A1 → A caused a one-pass root detector to put A at root rank. In #68 the author could not tell two groups of feeders apart. The supplied #68 roots are already written in groups, so target alignment and routing also matter. ZM fixed the root/grouping defects in #89. AM’s SVG order is derived from source-before-target constraints with cycle decisions and original-order ties. Its terminal suite already guards the exact #68 source and an interleaved variant. These reports justify distinguishing topology, authored narrative, and incidental insertion order during an ordering experiment. ## 3. A shipped crossing repair that required another row

**ZM #2656, opened November 4 and closed November 5, followed by merged #2859 on November 7.** This is a maintainer’s gallery reproduction for a five-entity ER diagram. ORDER links to LINE_ITEM and SHIPMENT. PRODUCT links to LINE_ITEM and REVIEW. The ASCII diagram had one crossing. A crossing-minimizing layer order by itself produced two *routed* crossings. A subsequent prototype achieved zero, then placed `receives` across the foreign `includes` shaft. The team initially documented the limit in #2665. [ZM #2656 and source](https://github.com/dfadler/zombie-mermaid/issues/2656) · [First rejected change](https://github.com/dfadler/zombie-mermaid/pull/2665)

The eventual renderer combined ELK layer and sibling order with per-box turn ordering, foreign-shaft-aware label placement, and extra room for a labelled gap. The exact before/after Unicode snapshots change from **62 columns × 16 rows to 61 × 17**, including the same two blank bottom rows. Occupied rows increase from 14 to 15. The canvas grows from 992 to 1,037 cells, or **4.54%**. The old `ships-via` run crosses the `includes` stem; the new drawing has four separately labelled lanes with zero shaft crossings. The PR separately reports about 5% more rows across 587 random diagrams. These are different measurements. An AM-like height-and-area veto would reject the exact dimensional trade in this accepted repair. The surface is terminal ER. The result supports investigating an explicit tradeoff in pixel Flowchart, without establishing its desirability for every graph. ## Checks on the improved AM seeds

On seed 135, AM default → balanced changes 13 → 10 proper crossings and 3 → 8 primitive pairs sharing positive-length collinear ink. On seed 139 the corresponding counts are 43 → 24 and 7 → 11. Seed 139’s eleven shared segment pairs represent **eight logical edge pairs**. In each balanced graph, three logical pairs share a same-direction prefix from the same source, and five share a same-direction suffix into the same target. Both branches of each common-source pair carry the bundle invariant. Every balanced overlap connects to that common source or target. The SDK reports zero same-pair parallel/opposing overlaps and zero soft label conflicts in both modes. Seed 139 retains an unverified paint result. [Emitted paths](../aesthetics.json) · [Pair-by-pair ownership and overlap lengths](raw/seed-shared-shafts.json)

This sharing can still be visually significant. On seed 139, n10 → n11 and n15 → n11 travel together for 360.296 px, with n8 → n11 joining the last 151.914 px before a single marker location. On seed 135, n7 → n8 and n7 → n3 start with 179.667 px of common trunk. I checked transitive connectivity as well as pairs, allowing a reader to switch routes at either boundary of every positive same-direction overlap and follow subsequent shafts without passing through a node. This introduced zero unauthored directed node pairs in either mode on either seed (26 distinct pairs for 135, 39 for 139). This test also returns zero false pairs on BM 1.1.3’s wallet, where two nearly aligned rails are only 0.741 px apart and the reader actually complained. Human traceability, near-touching painted lines and crowded tips remain distinct from exact-centerline reachability. ## Ranked recommendations

1. **Qualify crossing wins with a tracing task.** Keep the wallet’s asymmetric connectivity, the labelled three-sibling fan-out and the request/response as complementary controls. Check the lower wallet’s exclusive source, whether all three incoming arrows can be distinguished when a reader needs that, and whether every caption names a uniquely traceable branch. Preserve the legitimate trunk the #111 reporter asked for. For a zero-crossing incumbent with ambiguous same-direction ink, consider a targeted comparison only when this ownership evidence identifies a real problem. [Wallet](https://github.com/lukilabs/beautiful-mermaid/issues/52) · [Labelled fan control](https://github.com/lukilabs/beautiful-mermaid/issues/111)
2. **Keep source-aware defaults and evaluate placement with its actual ports, routes and labels.** The 34-node workflow is a strong positive control. On the 19-node witness, match any alternate feedback roles to their downstream docks and caption seats. Compare finished drawings. AM’s tested unforced strategy took 37 crossings to 48 and the proposed 36-crossing two-return change displaced a caption. ZM’s source-matched ER repair shows the value of coordinating layout and routing after an isolated improvement failed at paint. [Existing AM trials](/workspace/elk-layered-tools/issue619-sol-experiments-20261121/README.md) · [ZM’s rejected attempt](https://github.com/dfadler/zombie-mermaid/pull/2665) · [ZM’s accepted repair](https://github.com/dfadler/zombie-mermaid/pull/2859)
3. **Preserve the public balanced promise.** If readers need to spend a little canvas space to remove crossings or materially improve traceability, evaluate a separate, explicitly tradeoff-capable policy or an authorized ordinary-selector change. Show the before/after dimensions, counts, captions, markers and source-order consequences. The one-extra-row ER repair is an actual accepted trade. The seven-stage workflow illustrates a much larger one. Neither yields a generally applicable pixel or percentage threshold. [Balanced contract](/workspace/elk-layered-port/docs/api.md:167) · [ZM exact dimensional trade](raw/zm2859-exact-unicode-snapshots.json)
4. **Preserve bounded work and measure rejected attempts.** BM #64 has a downstream terminal viewer whose developer measured a 24-edge chain at 0.08 s and a 25-edge chain at 12.5 s followed by a crash. A five-second viewer timeout discarded the document, prompting a child-process workaround. An independent fork reproduction and bisect found a path-search cap fixed that trigger. The renderer and cause differ from AM’s, but this is direct evidence of the cost of unbounded search. The existing AM experiment already measured a rejected dense balanced case at +222 ms uncached warm median and up to +78.87 MiB paired peak RSS. Keep the shared two-extra-producer budget while testing a better candidate. [Viewer report](https://github.com/lukilabs/beautiful-mermaid/issues/64#issuecomment-6092322061) · [Fork bisect](https://github.com/lukilabs/beautiful-mermaid/issues/64#issuecomment-6128858430) · [AM timing and memory evidence](/workspace/elk-layered-tools/issue619-sol-experiments-20261121/README.md)
