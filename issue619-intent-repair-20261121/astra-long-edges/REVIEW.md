## What an ordinary split constructs

Let D be the sum of max(0, target rank − source rank − 1) over the actual, forward edge occurrences at the splitter entrance, after cycle handling, center-label insertion, ranking, high-degree/partition changes and hierarchy constraints. Every ordinary split inserts one dummy node, two ports and one continuation edge. The original edge survives with a new target. Ordinary expansion therefore adds D nodes, 2D ports and D edges. Authored rank declarations, counts after hyperedge merging, and output JSON omit information needed to reconstruct that total. Same-rank edges and adjacent ranks contribute zero. An unexpected earlier target rank is an error in this phase. [Rank assignment and preparation](/workspace/elk-layered-port/src/elk-layered/kernel/layout.ts:288)

## Order and projection counterexamples

- **Rank-by-rank insertion.** For A at rank 0 pointing to rank 4 and B at rank 1 pointing to rank 4, rank 1 visits its original B before the newly appended continuation of A. Rank 2 therefore receives B's dummy first. Materializing all of A's chain when visiting A reverses that order. Crossing minimization initializes some states using random draws per ordered occurrence. The existing saved-sweep optimization compresses reconstructible runs after preserving ordering and draws. Its guard differentiates ordinary split dummies from inverted-port dummies of the same type. [Saved sweep rows](/workspace/elk-layered-port/src/elk-layered/kernel/crossing.ts:1359)
- **Real target adjacency order.** On splitting, remove the old incoming occurrence from its target and append the continuation after its siblings. Replacing it in its old slot would change the result. Joining has the opposite local operation. It inserts the survivor immediately before the dropped occurrence at the ultimate target, then removes the dropped occurrence. A direct in-place replacement may save scans and splices on a proven distinct target roster, if join profiling warrants it. Keep ordered pairings when a merged dummy has multiple incoming and outgoing occurrences. [Split and join mutations](/workspace/elk-layered-port/src/elk-layered/kernel/long-edges.ts:69)
- **Captions, bends and markers belong to occurrences.** Parallel edges can carry different priorities, thicknesses or HEAD/TAIL/CENTER labels and share endpoint ports. Reversal swaps HEAD/TAIL placements. Moving a HEAD label preserves its original `endLabelEdge` reference, while center-label switching walks and can reorder dummy chains. The optional unnecessary-bendpoint mode emits the physical point at every joined dummy. Conflating records by node pair or shared `data` risks silently changing the section/label result. ## Sparse normalization is a separate algorithmic experiment

The current crossing sweep, Brandes–Köpf placement and orthogonal routing operate on every adjacent-rank occurrence. Placement explicitly detects inner segments between two LONG_EDGE nodes in consecutive layers. Sparse normalization changes the representation seen by those phases unless equivalent virtual visits, order, random consumption and numerical operations are supplied. An Eiglsperger-style implementation can be evaluated separately for actual time, peak memory, crossing count, labels, downstream geometric certification and output changes. Its published bounds concern its own algorithm and cannot certify exact coordinates for this port.
